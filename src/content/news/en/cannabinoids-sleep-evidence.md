Poor sleep is one of the main reasons people become curious about cannabinoids. What does the science say when all the studies are put together? The most complete answer is still the report by the **US National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine** (2017), and on this point it brings good news with some small print.

## What was done

- **Type of study:** an **evidence review** by an independent committee of experts. It is not a new experiment: it is an orderly look at what had been published up to then.
- **What was reviewed:** more than 10,000 scientific abstracts on the health effects of cannabis and cannabinoids, published since 1999.
- **How it was graded:** each conclusion carries a label for the strength of the evidence, from "conclusive" to "insufficient".

## What it found

- There is **moderate evidence** that cannabinoids, mainly nabiximols (a medicine containing THC and CBD), **improve short-term sleep** in people whose rest is disturbed by another condition.
- The four situations named in the report are **obstructive sleep apnoea**, **fibromyalgia**, **chronic pain** and **multiple sclerosis**.
- In almost all of those studies sleep was a secondary outcome: researchers were studying pain or spasticity, and better sleep came along with it.

## How to read it

"Moderate" is the third step out of five: there are trials pointing the same way, but not enough to be sure. On our scale that is a **C**. The report is about the short term and about medicines of known composition, not about any product. And it leaves out insomnia with no other cause, which had barely been studied: there the review does not say it fails, it says the trials are missing.

> When pain keeps you awake, easing the pain and sleeping better tend to arrive together.

## Why it matters to us

Because it brings order to a question usually answered with anecdotes. Knowing where there is evidence, and how good it is, lets people discuss the topic with their doctor with facts in hand. And it marks the path for what is left to study: trials designed to measure sleep, with longer follow-up.
