## What happened

Cannabinoids are increasingly used to help with sleep, but the science is not clear-cut. A reference review by the US National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2017) concludes that cannabinoids improve sleep in the short term for people whose rest is disrupted by pain, fibromyalgia, multiple sclerosis or sleep apnea.

There is, however, no good evidence for insomnia without an underlying cause, nor for long-term prolonged use. The foundation sells no products, gives no usage instructions and recommends trying nothing: it only relays what the studies say, with the source so you can check it yourself.

## Why it matters

Poor sleep affects physical and mental health, and many people look for alternatives when standard treatments do not work or cause side effects. Knowing in which cases the evidence is solid and in which it is not helps avoid false expectations and decisions based on sensational headlines.

For patients, families and health professionals, the difference between «it may help in some specific cases» and «it is a proven solution for insomnia» is not a detail: it shapes expectations and, ultimately, health decisions.

## Context

The evidence scale of the US National Academies runs from A (the science is clear) to E (there is not enough evidence). For cannabinoids and sleep, the assessment sits at an intermediate level: there are data, but not enough to be sure in every situation.

The product also matters: it is not the same to use an isolated cannabinoid in a clinical trial as to buy an oil over the counter. Dose, product type and medical supervision influence the outcome, and none of those factors are settled by current evidence.

## What we still don't know

It is not known whether the benefit persists with prolonged use, nor what the long-term effects are. It is also unclear which cannabinoids, at what doses, and for which profiles of people work best.

This information is educational and informative; it does not replace medical advice. If you have trouble sleeping, talk to a health professional before starting or stopping any treatment.
