The cannabis plant has more than a hundred known cannabinoids, and new ones are still turning up. **David Meiri's lab at the Technion** (Israel Institute of Technology), one of the groups that has studied cannabis and cancer most closely, has published in *Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy* the discovery of one with a chemical structure **unlike any known cannabinoid**, and with a very interesting effect.

## The background

About two in three breast cancers depend on oestrogen. One of the standard treatments is **tamoxifen**, taken for 5 or even 10 years. It works, but its side effects lead many patients to stop early. That is why anything that achieves the same effect with a lower dose is valuable.

## What was done

- **Study type:** **preclinical** research: breast cancer cells in the lab and then mice with oestrogen-dependent tumours.
- **How:** the team screened a collection of chemically distinct cannabis extracts, found one that made tumour cells more sensitive to tamoxifen and broke it down step by step until they found the compound responsible.

## What it found

- The compound, named **373.15b**, reproduces the effect of the whole extract on its own: **it makes tamoxifen work better**.
- On its own it is **not toxic** to the cells.
- It works by lowering the oestrogen receptor in tumour cells, through a signalling pathway called **Hippo**.
- In mice, 373.15b **reduced tumour volume and weight**, and the effect was stronger combined with tamoxifen.

## How to read it

This is a lab and animal discovery: it has not been tested in people yet, and that is the missing step. Its value is twofold: a new cannabinoid has been found, and it comes with a concrete mechanism that opens the door to **less tamoxifen for the same effect**, and so fewer side effects.

> The plant still holds compounds waiting to be discovered, and some may improve treatments we already have.

## Why it matters to us

This is exactly the kind of research we stand for: serious chemistry, an explained mechanism and a clear application. If human trials confirm it, it could help more patients complete their treatment.
