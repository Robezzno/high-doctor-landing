At the American Heart Association (AHA) annual meeting, held in Chicago from 6 to 9 November, a team from Tulane University presented an analysis of cannabis and cardiovascular health. The headline result: **more strokes among people who use cannabis, even occasionally**.

## What was done

- **Study type:** cross-sectional, using a US telephone health survey (BRFSS) from 2020 to 2024. It is a **snapshot**: it looks at use and disease at the same time, without following anyone over time.
- **Who:** 442,906 adults, average age 49, about half women, from the states that included cannabis questions.
- **How use was measured:** days of use in the past month, smoked, vaped or eaten: none, low (1–5 days), moderate (6–15) and heavy (16–30).
- **Adjustments:** tobacco, alcohol, obesity, diabetes, physical activity, education and income, among others.
- **Status:** a **conference poster, not yet peer reviewed**.

## What it found

- Compared with non-users, **low** use was linked to **23% higher odds** of having cardiovascular disease and **heavy** use to **21% higher odds**.
- The strongest signal was **stroke**: **40% higher odds** with low use and **25% higher** with heavy use.
- There was no statistically significant link with **heart attack**.
- The association was similar whether cannabis was smoked, vaped or eaten.

## What this study does not say

**It does not show that cannabis causes strokes.** As a snapshot, it cannot tell what came first. Use and diagnoses are **self-reported**, there is no data on potency or amount, and the "no use" group may include people who quit precisely after falling ill. Occasional use looking worse than daily use is odd and suggests factors that were not controlled for.

> An association in a survey is a warning sign to investigate, not a conclusion.

## Why it matters to us

It is not a one-off: a 2024 study in the *Journal of the American Heart Association*, using the same survey, had already found more strokes and heart attacks among daily users. Several signals pointing the same way justify asking about cannabis, just as doctors ask about tobacco, when assessing cardiovascular risk, as AHA expert Robert Page argues. And if you take heart medication, do not change it because of a news story: talk to your doctor.
