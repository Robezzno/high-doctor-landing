Chronic pain is one of the most common reasons medical cannabis is prescribed. What was missing was **what happens over time**: whether the improvement lasts, and why some people do better than others. An analysis of the UK Medical Cannabis Registry, published in the *Journal of Clinical Pharmacology*, followed 3,744 patients for two years.

## What was done

- **Study type:** observational cohort. It uses data from real patients whose doctors had already prescribed cannabis-based medicinal products in the UK, where they have been legal on prescription since 2018.
- **Who:** 3,744 people whose main reason for treatment was chronic pain.
- **What was measured:** pain severity and how much it interferes with daily life, quality of life, anxiety and sleep, using validated questionnaires, from baseline up to 24 months.
- **What is new:** the team grouped patients by how their pain evolved to see which profiles respond best.

## What it found

- **Every outcome improved** from baseline: pain severity, pain interference, quality of life, anxiety and sleep.
- **More than half** of patients reached an improvement in pain large enough to matter in daily life (a minimal clinically important difference).
- **Five distinct patterns** emerged and **all five ended with less pain than at the start**, although some improved much more than others.
- The best predictors of response were not the product or the dose, but **how well the person slept, their anxiety level and the type of pain** at baseline.
- About 14% reported an adverse event, mostly mild or moderate. According to NORML's coverage, roughly one in four people taking opioids also managed to cut them down meaningfully.

## How to read it

This is a **real-world** study with no placebo comparison group, so it cannot fully separate the effect of cannabis from other factors; the authors call for controlled trials to confirm it. Its value lies in its size, the two-year follow-up and something very practical: it helps understand **who** it may work best for.

> The question is no longer just whether medical cannabis helps with pain, but for whom and how.

## Why it matters to us

In Spain, medical cannabis is now regulated (Royal Decree 903/2025) and dispensed through hospital pharmacies on a specialist's prescription. Long-term follow-up data like these, from a country with years of experience, are exactly what doctors and patients need to make informed decisions.
