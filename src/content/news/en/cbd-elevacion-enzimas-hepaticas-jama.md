## What happened

A **randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial** published in **JAMA Internal Medicine** (July 2025) assessed the hepatic safety of cannabidiol (CBD) at doses representative of consumer use of unregulated CBD products.

The study enrolled **201 healthy adults** (median age 36 years) for 28 days. The CBD group received **5 mg/kg/day** (2.5 mg/kg twice daily) using the FDA-approved prescription product (Epidiolex) to avoid contaminants found in unregulated products. The control group received placebo.

**Key finding:** 8 of 151 participants (5.6%; 95% CI: 1.8–9.3%) in the CBD group had alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) elevations **greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal**. **Zero** of 50 placebo participants met this criterion. Seven participants met withdrawal criteria for potential drug-induced liver injury (DILI), detected at days 21–28.

No differences were observed between CBD and placebo groups for endocrine hormones (total testosterone and inhibin B in men; TSH, total triiodothyronine, and free thyroxine in all participants).

## Why it matters

Consumer use of unregulated CBD products has surged; many users take **200–400 mg/day** (or more) chronically. Until now, most hepatotoxicity evidence came from high-dose prescription CBD (up to 25 mg/kg/day in epilepsy). This trial fills the gap: **at real-world consumer doses, CBD also raises transaminases in a non-trivial proportion of healthy people**.

The elevation appeared late (weeks 3–4) and without symptoms. The foundation sells no products, gives no usage instructions, and recommends nothing: it only relays what the study says, with the source so you can check it yourself.

## Context

- **Dose used:** 5 mg/kg/day ≈ 350–400 mg/day in a 70–80 kg adult, at the high end of reported consumer use of unregulated products.
- **Product:** Epidiolex (pharmaceutical-grade purified CBD). Over-the-counter products may contain contaminants, inaccurate labeling, or other cannabinoids that alter the safety profile.
- **Population:** healthy adults, non-smokers, no concomitant medications, no significant alcohol use. Results may differ in patients with pre-existing liver disease, obesity, polypharmacy, or older age.
- **Evidence level:** phase I/II randomized clinical trial, double-blind, placebo-controlled, registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06192589). Published in a high-impact journal (JAMA Internal Medicine).

## What we still don't know

- What happens with use over **months or years**: in the trial, values returned to normal one to two weeks after stopping CBD, but it lasted only 28 days.
- What happens at **lower doses** (e.g., 50–150 mg/day), more common in self-medication.
- Safety in **vulnerable populations** (liver disease, pregnancy, drug interactions).
- Effect of **full-spectrum products** (with THC, terpenes, other cannabinoids) vs. isolated CBD.
