**Burning mouth syndrome** is chronic pain, like a scald on the tongue, lips or palate, with no visible injury to explain it. It mostly affects women after menopause, and existing treatments only half work. A team at the **Federal University of Paraná** (Curitiba, Brazil) tested CBD oil in a placebo-controlled trial published in *Oral Surgery, Oral Medicine, Oral Pathology and Oral Radiology*, and the results are encouraging.

## What was done

- **Study type:** a **randomized, placebo-controlled** clinical trial at an orofacial pain clinic.
- **Who took part:** 26 patients (96% women, average age 62), randomly split: 13 took full-spectrum CBD oil and 13 an identical-looking placebo.
- **What was measured:** pain intensity and how much it interfered with daily life, oral health-related quality of life (the OHIP-14 questionnaire) and adverse events.

## What it found

- In the CBD group, pain **dropped clearly over the weeks**, both in intensity and in how much it got in the way of daily life. The placebo group stayed where it was.
- The difference between the two groups became significant **from the third assessment onward** and held.
- **Oral quality of life improved** significantly on CBD.
- **There were no serious adverse events.**

## How to read it

It is a small, single-blind trial (patients did not know what they were taking, but the team did), and it used a full-spectrum oil, so it is not clear which plant compounds do the work. The authors themselves call for larger trials. Even so, a placebo comparison is exactly what this field often lacks, and here the difference was clear.

> In a condition with few options, a clean result against placebo is good news.

## Why it matters to us

Burning mouth syndrome is little known, but people who have it can live with pain for years. A well-tolerated treatment that reduces pain and improves quality of life deserves serious study, and this trial lays the groundwork for it.
